Introduction to Color Theory

Color theory is a set of principles used to understand how colors work together, how they are created, and how they influence perception. Designers, artists, and content creators use color theory to select palettes that create balance, contrast, and harmony. These principles explain how colors relate on the color wheel, how they mix, and how combinations shape the way people interpret a design.

Key Color Theory Terms

  1. Hue— The pure form of a color without tint, shade, or tone.
  2. Color wheel— A circular arrangement that shows relationships between hues.
  3. Primary colors— Colors that cannot be created by mixing other colors: red, blue, and yellow.
  4. Secondary colors— Colors made by mixing two primary colors, such as green, orange, and purple.
  5. Tertiary colors— Colors made by mixing a primary color with an adjacent secondary color.
  6. Tint— A lighter version of a color created by adding white.
  7. Shade— A darker version of a color created by adding black.
  8. Tone— A color modified by adding gray.
  9. Saturation— The intensity or purity of a color.
  10. Value— The lightness or darkness of a color.
  11. Warm colors— Colors that feel warm, such as red, orange, and yellow.
  12. Cool colors— Colors that feel cool, such as blue, green, and purple.
  13. Complementary colors— Opposite colors on the wheel that create strong contrast.
  14. Analogous colors— Colors next to each other on the wheel that create harmony.
  15. Triadic colors— Three colors evenly spaced around the color wheel.
  16. Monochromatic colors— Variations of a single color using tints, tones, and shades.
  17. Split-complementary— A color scheme using one color and two colors adjacent to its complement.
  18. Color harmony— A pleasing arrangement of colors that creates balance.
  19. Contrast— The difference between colors that makes elements stand out.
  20. Color temperature— The perceived warmth or coolness of a color.
  21. Neutral colors— Colors such as black, white, gray, and beige that are less intense.
  22. Opacity— The degree to which a color blocks light.
